Cybersecurity Crisis: The Last 3 Months Recap (Nov 2025 - Feb 2026)

📅 February 12, 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read 🔖 Threat Intelligence

The cybersecurity landscape has witnessed unprecedented chaos over the past three months. From record-breaking credential leaks to sophisticated state-sponsored espionage campaigns, this period marks a critical inflection point in how attackers operate. This comprehensive breakdown covers the major incidents, emerging threat patterns, and lessons every organization must learn.

🔴 Major Incidents Breakdown

1. The 16 Billion Password Leak (June 2025)

Impact: Catastrophic • Over 16 billion user credentials from Google, Apple, and Facebook were leaked in what security researchers called one of the largest credential-stuffing dumps ever recorded. This aggregated data from infostealer malware and previous breaches, emphasizing the critical need for Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) across all platforms.

2. SK Telecom Breach: 27 Million Users Compromised

Attack Vector: Remote Access Trojan (RAT) • Attackers deployed BPFDoor, a sophisticated malware leveraging Berkeley Packet Filter (BPF) hooks to intercept and manipulate network traffic on Linux servers. The breach exposed SIM management data, IMSI numbers, and authentication keys for 27 million users.

3. Red Hat GitLab Catastrophe: 570GB of Data Stolen

Threat Actor: Crimson Collective • Over 28,000 internal repositories were breached, exposing 570GB of compressed data including VPN settings, API keys, and credentials for enterprises like IBM, American Express, Cisco, NSA, and the Department of Defence. This supply chain exposure represents one of 2025's most damaging incidents.

4. Qantas Breach: 5.7 Million Records

Root Cause: Salesforce Third-Party Integration Vulnerability • Attackers exploited misconfigured Salesforce systems to exfiltrate 5.7 million customer records including names, emails, phone numbers, and frequent-flyer data. This incident highlights the persistent Salesforce supply chain vulnerability affecting organizations across industries.

5. Healthcare Under Siege

Healthcare facilities were heavily targeted in Q4 2025-Q1 2026. Notable breaches included Yale New Haven Health (resulting in an $18 million settlement), affecting 1.9 million patients with exposed medical records, insurance information, and personal identifiers.

⚠️ Emerging Threat Patterns

1. Trust Abuse Over Brute Force

Attackers are shifting from loud, infrastructure-focused attacks to sophisticated trust abuse and perception manipulation. This includes weaponizing incomplete test data (e.g., NordVPN development environment claims) and exploiting legitimate administrative functions within cloud services.

2. Messaging Platforms as Attack Vectors

Russia-aligned UAC-0184 and other threat actors are leveraging Viber, Signal, and WhatsApp as primary delivery channels for malware like Hijack Loader and Remcos RAT. Traditional email-only defenses are now insufficient.

3. Ransomware Intensification

January 2026 alone recorded 678 ransomware attacks—a 10% increase year-over-year. Groups like Gentlemen, Sinobi, Everest, and Milkyway are operating with sophisticated evasion techniques and dual-extortion tactics (encryption + data exfiltration).

4. Critical Vulnerability Chains

Multiple zero-day and critical CVEs were exploited in January alone:

  • CVE-2026-20274 - Cisco Unified Communications Manager RCE
  • CVE-2026-20860 - VMware Aria Suite RCE (actively exploited)
  • CVE-2025-38067 - Microsoft Office zero-day
  • CVE-2025-3421 - vm2 NodeJS sandbox escape

💡 Key Takeaways for Organizations

  • Mandatory MFA: 16 billion credentials in circulation demands universal MFA implementation
  • Supply Chain Vigilance: Third-party integrations (especially Salesforce) require continuous monitoring
  • Behavioral Detection: Email controls alone won't catch sophisticated attacks via messaging apps
  • Patch Management: Rapid patching is critical with multiple active zero-days in circulation
  • Incident Response: Average detection time must decrease—lateral movement happens fast

This recap underscores that 2026 will demand more sophisticated defenses, continuous threat intelligence, and a shift from perimeter-focused to behavior-focused security strategies. Organizations that adapt will survive; those that don't will become statistics.
